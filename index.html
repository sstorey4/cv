About

I study how scientists and engineers build, test, and maintain software, and how that understanding can make research code more reliable.

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, and a graduate of the University of Georgia. I work as a graduate research assistant in the SAIL lab under Dr. Nasir Eisty, where my work centers on scientific software development and testing in domain-specific fields. My broader interests include mining software repositories, collaborative development practices, and the reliability of large language models.

Current Projects

01

Why Not WONTFIX?

Submitted · MSR

Research paper · Mining software repositories

An empirical study of the WONTFIX resolution in issue trackers. Using data mining across software projects, the paper examines when and why teams decide not to fix reported bugs, and how that decision shapes collaboration and development within software teams.

  • Data Mining
  • Issue Tracking
  • Team Dynamics
  • Empirical SE
02

UT Campus Parking Predictor

In Development

Web application · Predictive modeling

A website for the University of Tennessee campus that uses historical parking citation data to predict when and where it is best to park. The model surfaces enforcement patterns by lot, day, and time so students and staff can make better-informed parking decisions.

  • Predictive Analytics
  • Open Data
  • Web Development
03

Conflict Injection in LLMs

In Progress

Research · Large language models

A study of how large language models respond when the information they are given contradicts itself. By deliberately injecting conflicts into provided context, the project measures whether models detect, resolve, or silently propagate inconsistencies, with implications for building more reliable LLM-based systems.

  • LLM Evaluation
  • Robustness
  • Knowledge Conflicts
